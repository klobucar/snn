L354:   *     * #### Voter Registration
L355: Ellsworth residents may register to vote at the City Clerk’s Office at City Hall during regular business hours. Proof of residency and proof of identity are required. A physical street address in Ellsworth (not a post office box mailing address/or location such as the UPS may be used) must be demonstrated. If your Maine Driver’s License shows the correct physical address that will satisfy both requirements.
L356: IF you do not have a driver’s license Maine allows you to use any of the following for identification to register to vote:
L357: Government-issued photograph identification document or credential. A government-issued photograph identification document or credential, including, but not limited to, a current and valid United States passport, military identification, driver’s license or state identification; or Other government-issued identification document.
L358: A government-issued identification document without a photograph, including, but not limited to, a certified birth certificate or a signed social security card; or Other official documents.
L359:  An official document, including, but not limited to, a document confirming eligibility determinations for public benefits, a utility bill, a bank statement, a government check, a paycheck or other government document that shows the name and address of the voter; or Maine ID or Driver’s License number, or last four of your social security number, if it is already in our computer system.
L360: Maine allows you to use An official document, including, but not limited to, a document confirming eligibility determinations for public benefits, a utility bill, a bank statement, a government check, a paycheck or other government document that shows the name and address of the voter to provide proof of physical residency for the purpose of registering to vote.
L361: If you are currently registered to vote and wish to change any information pertinent to your voting record, such as your name or address, visit the City Clerk’s office or mail the new information to: City of Ellsworth, City Clerk’s Office, 1 City Hall Plaza, Ellsworth ME 04605.

L363:     * #### Polling Hours, Locations & Wards
L364: 
L365: Municipal elections are held on Tuesday following the first Monday in November. During each municipal election, all positions are up for election.
L366: The State of Maine general and referendum elections are held annually on Tuesday following the first Monday in November. The primary election is held on the 2nd Tuesday of June of each general election year.
L367: 
L368: Regular polling hours are open at 8:00 am and close at 8:00 pm. During Presidential Elections the City Council will often authorize poles to open at 7 am.
L369: 
L370: Absentee Ballots must be turned in prior to 8:00 p.m. on Election Day to be counted.
L371: There are 4 polling place locations within the City of Ellsworth. Voters are required to vote at the appropriate polling place location for their ward. Please contact the City Clerk’s Office at City Hall for the location of your ward.
L372:       * cite54†Ward One votes in the Upstairs Auditorium at Ellsworth City Hall, 1 City Hall Plaza, and covers the entire downtown area on the east of the Union River.
L373:       * cite55†Ward Two votes at Your Place Community Center, 4 Old Mill Road, behind Sunrise Glass.
L374:       * cite56†Ward Three votes in the Gymnasium at the Maine Coast Baptist Church, 867 Bangor Road, on Route 1A and covers north Ellsworth.
L375:       * cite54†Ward Four votes in the Council Chambers at Ellsworth City Hall, 1 City Hall Plaza, and covers the Bridge Hill area west of the Union River.

L379: 
L380:       * cite58†State of Maine Absentee Voting Information†absenteeballotrequest.sos.maine.gov L381:       * cite59†How to In-Person Absentee Vote Video L382: The state law permits absentee ballots to be cast at any election by any voter who properly requests a ballot. If a voter is interested in voting in a particular election, but is unable to go to the polling place on Election Day, then the voter should obtain an absentee ballot.
L383: Voters may vote by absentee ballot at City Hall. Office hours are Monday through Thursday, 7:00am – 6:00pm. Before each major election, the City Clerk’s Department also offers special hours to correct the voter’s list and conduct absentee voting. Please call 207-667-2563 to verify dates and times.

An immediate family member (on behalf of the voter) may, in writing, request a ballot be sent by mail or delivered to the voter by that family member. Applications for an absentee ballot may also be downloadable through this web site as an election draws near.
L385: If the ballot is returned by another immediate family member, the other immediate family member must also sign the application or written request indicating they returned the ballot. The family member must state his/her relationship to the voter.

L392:   * ### Polling Consolidation into One Location
L393: The City of Ellsworth is considering a proposal to consolidate all four of its wards into a single polling location for Election Day. This recommendation comes after careful evaluation of the logistical challenges and costs associated with the current system. Consolidating the wards into one central polling location would provide several significant benefits, both in terms of cost savings and operational efficiency.
L394: 
L395: Reasons for Consolidation
L396:     1. Cost Savings
L397: One of the main advantages of consolidating polling locations is the substantial reduction in operational costs. Each ward currently requires separate staffing, equipment, and supplies. By consolidating the polling sites into one central location, the city can eliminate the need to rent and maintain four separate polling sites. This would lead to direct savings on facility rental, staffing, and materials. Fewer locations mean fewer expenses, allowing resources to be redirected to other important areas.
L398:     2. Optimized Use of Staff
L399: A single polling location allows for better utilization of staff. Instead of spreading election workers thinly across multiple locations, a centralized polling site allows the city to deploy staff more effectively. With all workers at one site, the city can better train personnel, reduce the risk of miscommunication, and ensure that workers are fully focused on their tasks. This would result in improved efficiency, fewer mistakes, and a smoother operation overall.
L400:     3. Simplified Oversight and Coordination
L401: Having all voters at one location simplifies the logistical challenges of managing the election process. It allows city officials to better oversee the event, ensuring all materials and equipment are in one place, reducing the potential for errors or misplacement. A single location also makes it easier to respond to any issues or concerns quickly, ensuring that voters have a seamless experience. This streamlined approach enhances transparency and enables more effective management.
L402:     4. Increased Efficiency and Voter Accessibility
L403: Consolidating polling locations would improve the overall efficiency of Election Day operations. Voters would no longer need to navigate between multiple polling sites, reducing confusion and wait times. While concerns regarding accessibility would be carefully addressed, a single location would be more straightforward for voters, making the process easier for everyone involved. It would also reduce the chances of voters mistakenly going to the wrong location.
L404: Consolidating the polling locations into a single site for Election Day provides numerous advantages, including significant cost savings, improved staff utilization, and more efficient oversight. These benefits will not only streamline the election process but also improve the experience for both voters and city staff. By centralizing operations, the City of Ellsworth can ensure that its elections are run more efficiently and effectively.

L445:   * ## Election Worker Signup
L446: The City of Ellsworth is looking for citizens that are willing to work during State and Local Elections. The City has 4 wards that require a certain level of staffing to assist voters each election. The duties include welcoming voters, registering new voters, handling ballots and preparing end of night tally sheets. State elections are the 2nd Tuesday’s in June and November. City elections are normally running concurrent with the State, with an occasional special election as needed.

L37: ## Voter Registration
L38: 
L39: Are you registered to vote? You can register at any time, either at City Hall or at the election. While we do register voters at the election, it can mean waiting in line for a while.
L40: 
L41: If you are not sure if you are registered, please call the City Clerks department: (207) 582-2223.
L42: 
L43: To register you need a valid ID and proof of residency. If your driver’s license does not show a valid Gardiner address, we will accept mail, a pay stub or a rent receipt/lease agreement.

L46: ## Descriptions of Elections
L47: 
L48: ### State Primary Election
L49: 
L50: Primary Elections determine each qualified party’s nomination of candidates for federal offices and State and County offices. Party candidates who are elected at the Primary qualify to appear on the November General Election ballot.
L51: 
L52: ### General Election
L53: 
L54: The General Election is held nationally on the first Tuesday following the first Monday in November.
L55: ### Referendum Election
L56: 
L57: Referendum Elections are held to provide Maine's citizens an opportunity to vote on People's Veto Referenda, Direct Initiatives of Legislation (i.e. Citizen Initiatives), Bond Issues, other referenda proposed by the Legislature, and Constitutional Amendments. Referendum elections are an important part of the heritage of public participation in Maine.
L58: ### Maine Political Parties
L59: 
L60: Maine currently has three qualified parties: Democratic, Green Independent and Republican that may participate in primary elections. (Note: although some unenrolled candidates choose to designate themselves as “Independent,” there is no “Independent” party in Maine.)
L61: ### Ranked-Choice Voting (RCV)
L62: 
L63: Ranked-choice voting will be used in the 2024 State Primary and General elections. Visit our RCV Resources page for more information about ranked-choice voting, including Frequently Asked Questions and sample ballots.
