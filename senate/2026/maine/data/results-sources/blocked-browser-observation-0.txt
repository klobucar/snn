L41: 
L42: The City Clerk’s Office serves as the official record keeper for the City of Bath, ensuring transparent and accessible local government. The office manages City Council records, elections, licenses and permits, public notices, vital records, and official City documents. The Clerk also supports boards and committees, maintains the City Charter and ordinances, and provides public access to municipal records in accordance with state law.
L43:   * cite10†City Charter L44:   * cite11†City Ordinances L45:   * cite12†Street Book(PDF, 69MB) L46: Darci Wheeler was appointed City Clerk by the City Council and took the Oath of Office on January 8, 2020. She is a member of the Maine Town and City Clerks Association (MTCCA), the New England Association of City and Town Clerks (NEACTC), and the International Institute of Municipal Clerks (IIMC). She earned her Certified Clerk of Maine (CCM) certificate in 2022 and her Master Municipal Clerk (MMC) designation from IIMC in 2024.
L47: She is joined by Amanda Yenco, Assistant City Clerk and General Assistance Administrator.

L182: Why is the Recall Committee Receiving Additional Time?
L183: 
L184: Following completion of the signature validation process, the City consulted with legal counsel regarding how to administer the next step of the recall process.
L185: The City Charter contains language that required legal interpretation regarding whether a petition that is determined to be insufficient for lack of valid signatures may be supplemented with additional signatures. After reviewing the Charter and obtaining legal guidance, the City determined that the Recall Committee will be provided the opportunity to file supplemental petitions as provided in Section 5.10(b)(1) of the Charter.
L186: The legal advice recognized that there were differing interpretations of the Charter language, and the City chose the interpretation that allows the Charter process to continue while preserving the rights of all parties.
L187: Administering the Recall Process
L188: 
L189: The recall process is administered by the City Clerk’s Office in accordance with the City Charter. Validating hundreds of petition signatures for multiple recall petitions is a detailed and time-intensive process that requires careful review to ensure every signature is evaluated consistently and fairly.
L190: The City Clerk’s Office dedicated significant time and attention to this work while continuing to meet the day-to-day needs of residents and the many responsibilities of administering elections and City business. Throughout the process, staff worked diligently to uphold both the letter and the intent of the Charter while ensuring the process remained fair, impartial, and transparent for everyone involved.
L191: The City sincerely appreciates the professionalism, integrity, and commitment demonstrated by the Clerk’s Office throughout this process.

L200: Next Steps
L201: 
L202: Following the supplemental petition deadline, the City Clerks will validate the newly submitted signatures to determine whether enough valid signatures have been received to trigger a recall election. No additional supplemental signatures will be accepted after the deadline.
L203: If the Clerks determine that the required threshold of 1,279 valid signatures has been met for a petition, they will proceed with scheduling a recall election in accordance with the City Charter. The recall election will provide voters at large in the city with the opportunity to decide whether the councilor named in that petition should be recalled.
L204: Additional information about the recall process from the City Charter is below. The City will also soon provide further details about the election process, including information on how City business would continue if the outcome of one or more recall elections were to temporarily reduce the number of seated councilors below the quorum required to conduct Council business.

L354:   *     * #### Voter Registration
L355: Ellsworth residents may register to vote at the City Clerk’s Office at City Hall during regular business hours. Proof of residency and proof of identity are required. A physical street address in Ellsworth (not a post office box mailing address/or location such as the UPS may be used) must be demonstrated. If your Maine Driver’s License shows the correct physical address that will satisfy both requirements.
L356: IF you do not have a driver’s license Maine allows you to use any of the following for identification to register to vote:
L357: Government-issued photograph identification document or credential. A government-issued photograph identification document or credential, including, but not limited to, a current and valid United States passport, military identification, driver’s license or state identification; or Other government-issued identification document.
L358: A government-issued identification document without a photograph, including, but not limited to, a certified birth certificate or a signed social security card; or Other official documents.
L359:  An official document, including, but not limited to, a document confirming eligibility determinations for public benefits, a utility bill, a bank statement, a government check, a paycheck or other government document that shows the name and address of the voter; or Maine ID or Driver’s License number, or last four of your social security number, if it is already in our computer system.
L360: Maine allows you to use An official document, including, but not limited to, a document confirming eligibility determinations for public benefits, a utility bill, a bank statement, a government check, a paycheck or other government document that shows the name and address of the voter to provide proof of physical residency for the purpose of registering to vote.
L361: If you are currently registered to vote and wish to change any information pertinent to your voting record, such as your name or address, visit the City Clerk’s office or mail the new information to: City of Ellsworth, City Clerk’s Office, 1 City Hall Plaza, Ellsworth ME 04605.

L363:     * #### Polling Hours, Locations & Wards
L364: 
L365: Municipal elections are held on Tuesday following the first Monday in November. During each municipal election, all positions are up for election.
L366: The State of Maine general and referendum elections are held annually on Tuesday following the first Monday in November. The primary election is held on the 2nd Tuesday of June of each general election year.
L367: 
L368: Regular polling hours are open at 8:00 am and close at 8:00 pm. During Presidential Elections the City Council will often authorize poles to open at 7 am.
L369: 
L370: Absentee Ballots must be turned in prior to 8:00 p.m. on Election Day to be counted.
L371: There are 4 polling place locations within the City of Ellsworth. Voters are required to vote at the appropriate polling place location for their ward. Please contact the City Clerk’s Office at City Hall for the location of your ward.
L372:       * cite54†Ward One votes in the Upstairs Auditorium at Ellsworth City Hall, 1 City Hall Plaza, and covers the entire downtown area on the east of the Union River.
L373:       * cite55†Ward Two votes at Your Place Community Center, 4 Old Mill Road, behind Sunrise Glass.
L374:       * cite56†Ward Three votes in the Gymnasium at the Maine Coast Baptist Church, 867 Bangor Road, on Route 1A and covers north Ellsworth.
