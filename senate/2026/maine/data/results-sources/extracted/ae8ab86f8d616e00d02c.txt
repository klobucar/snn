ARTICLE TWO: The Town hereby ordains to amend the Emergency Management Ordinance by
adding the Disaster Assistance Fund to Section 7.E.


 Statement of Fact: The purpose of this amendment is to incorporate the Town of
 York's Disaster Assistance Fund into the Emergency Management Ordinance. The
 Disaster Assistance Fund was created and put into use after the most recent Emergency
 Management Ordinance amendment was adopted. The fund is funded from specific
 categories of FEMA reimbursements, pursuant to the Disaster Assistance Fund Policy,
 which was adopted by the Selectboard on March 11, 2024.

                                   Selectboard Recommendation:
                                            5 - In Favor
                                            0 - Opposed



Proposed Amendment: Amend Section 7 Financial Authority as follows:

SECTION 7: FINANCIAL AUTHORITY

E.     Local Spending Authority.

       1. Operating Budgets. To the extent expenses during and after an emergency can be charged to
          approved operating budgets, they shall be. Typically this will include costs of fuel, labor, and
          other costs which occur regardless of the emergency.

       2. Supplementary Contingency. The Selectboard may vote to utilize funds available in
          Supplementary Contingency. The authorizing warrant article sets the amount and means of
          utilization.

       3. Disaster Assistance Fund. At the request of The Director or any Department Head, the
          Selectboard may vote to utilize funds available in the Disaster Assistance Fund. The Disaster
          Assistance Fund Policy sets the means of utilization not to exceed the amount available in the

          funding to properly respond to an emergency, it may call for a Special Budget Referendum in
          accordance with the Town of York Home Rule Charter. This would be likely if costs are
          likely to exceed the amount of Supplementary Contingency and external financial assistance.

ARTICLE FOUR: The Town hereby ordains to enact an Ordinance titled “Revenue-Bond
Preference for Self-Supporting Capital Projects” as submitted by citizen petition.

  Statement of Fact: The purpose of this article is to require that municipal capital projects
  to be funded with bonded debt and deemed by the Selectboard and Budget Committee
  to be reasonably capable of supporting their own operating and debt-service costs
  through fees, rentals, or other dedicated revenues be financed through the issuance of
  revenue bonds rather than that of property-tax-backed general-obligation bonds.

                                    Selectboard Recommendation:
                                             0 - In Favor
                                             5 - Opposed
Section 1. Purpose and Intent
York's long-term fiscal health benefits from a clear distinction in the funding of maintaining what it needs
and adding what it merely wants. Capital investments essential to safety, legal compliance, or core
municipal functions constitute needs and merit stable, tax-supported funding and timely investment. By
contrast, wants-capital investments intended to enhance quality of life or convenience but not required for
core operations or compliance-should, wherever possible, be self-sustaining so as to mitigate the
compounding effect on the property-tax burden of York's citizens.

To that end, the Town of York maintains a policy of funding needed capital investments with either
property-tax-backed General Obligation Bonds or Revenue Bonds or a hybrid, and prefers, whenever
feasible, to fund wanted capital investments with Revenue Bonds, which are backed by project-generated
revenues arising from use or operation. These revenues may include user fees, charges, rents,
assessments, comparable payments, or other dedicated non-tax sources. This ordinance establishes the
criteria governing the issuance of such Revenue Bonds in lieu of General Obligation bonds by the Town.

Section 2. Definitions
a) The term "Revenue-Producing Facility" refers to any business, utility, structure, or improvement that
is reasonably able to generate specific income sufficient to pay its operating costs and debt obligations by
imposing user fees, rents, or other charges for its use or operation.

b) "Reasonably be expected to generate, or be capable of generating, revenue from use or
operation" means that the proposed facility, improvement, or system is of a type that ordinarily yields
income through user fees, rentals, service charges, or comparable receipts, and that, based on customary
practice and the information required under Section 4 (Feasibility Report), it is intended, designed, or
structurally suited to generate such revenue sufficient to contribute toward meeting operating costs and
debt obligations. This standard does not require guaranteed profitability, but it does require a good-faith,
evidence-based showing, as documented in the feasibility report prepared under Section 4(1), that the
project meets the characteristics of a revenue-producing facility as defined in 30-A M.R.S.A. §5402(2).

c) A “Revenue Bond" is a type of bond issued under the Municipal Revenue-Producing Facilities Act,
30-A M.R.S.A. §§5401- 5420 and is a type of municipal bond that is repaid solely from the revenues
generated by the specific project or facility it finances, rather than from property taxes or the general
taxing authority of the municipality.

d) A "General-Obligation Bond" is a bond supported by the Town's commitment, including its taxing
authority and full financial backing.

e) A "Hybrid Bond" is a debt instrument that combines features of both (d) and (e).

Section 3. Applicability
This ordinance applies to proposed capital projects undertaken for the acquisition, construction, or
improvement of:

(1) recreation, community, or cultural facilities that the Selectboard and Budget Committee deem may
reasonably be expected to generate, or are capable of generating, revenue from their use or operation,
including, but not limited to, user fees, charges, rents, assessments, or similar payments;

(2) parking, harbor, water, sewer, solid-waste, or telecommunications systems; and

(3) any other facilities that constitute revenue-producing entities under the Municipal Revenue-Producing
Facilities Act, 30-A M.R.S.A. §5401-5420.

In the event of ambiguity concerning the classification of a facility or project, this Section shall be
construed in a manner consistent with the Purpose stated in Section 1 and, where not proscribed by law,
shall favor the classification of qualifying facilities as revenue-producing.

Section 4. Required Feasibility Analysis
Prior to submitting any such project for financing consideration, the Town Manager or a designated
representative shall prepare for the Selectboard and Budget Committee a Revenue
Feasibility Report that details:

(1) Projected gross revenues, operating expenses, and net operating income.

(2) A Debt Service Coverage Ratio (DSCR) of no less than 1.25x using conservative revenue estimates.

(3) A sensitivity analysis demonstrating the project's capacity to service debt in the event of a 10%
reduction in revenues.

(4) Confirmation of legal eligibility pursuant to the provisions of the Municipal Revenue-Producing
Facilities Act, 30-A M.R.S.A. §5401-5420.

Section 5. Financing Determination
(a) Revenue Bond Determination. If the Revenue Feasibility Report called for by Section 4
demonstrates that expected net revenues are sufficient to meet or exceed the coverage ratio outlined in
Subsection (2) above, the Budget Committee shall establish Revenue Bonds as the sole funding method
for the project.

(b) General-Obligation or Hybrid Determination. If the Revenue Feasibility Report called for by
Section 4 demonstrates that net revenues are inadequate, the Budget Committee may, by majority vote,
establish General-Obligation or hybrid financing as the specific funding method for the project. The
Committee shall provide an accompanying written statement detailing the necessity and rationale for this
determination.

Section 6. Voter Oversight
If the overall project cost exceeds the amount that triggers a charter-required referendum, the ballot
question must specify both the type of bond to be issued and the source of repayment funds. For example:
"Shall the Town of York authorize revenue bonds, to be repaid solely from user fees and rentals, for

ARTICLE FIVE: The Town hereby ordains to enact an Ordinance titled “Stop ALPR’s” as
submitted by citizen petition.

  Statement of Fact: The purpose of this ordinance is to prohibit the use of ALPR camera
  systems across any York Town Departments without the majority approval of the voters.

                                    Selectboard Recommendation:
                                             4 - In Favor
                                             1 - Opposed

Section 1 Title
This ordinance shall be known and may be cited as Stop ALPRs Ordinance.

Section 2 Purpose
The purpose of this ordinance is to prohibit the use of ALPR camera systems across any York Town
Departments without the majority approval of the voters. The Automated License Plate Reader (ALPR) is
a technology that enables drag-net surveillance in public areas which imposes on 4th Amendment
privileges guaranteed by the Bill of Rights under the Constitution. The company acting in York is Flock
Safety, which has been collecting data since 2025 in this town.

The use of ALPRs has the potential to negatively impact the residents in York at the expense of their own
tax dollars. The existing Flock Safety surveillance system shall be removed completely in compliance
with Section 4. ALPRs shall require public approval before any new contract is signed with the Town of
York in any Town Department.

Section 3 Definitions
ALPRs: For the purpose of this Ordinance, the term ALPR means Automated License Plate Reader;
which will assume the inclusion of any Artificial Intelligence incorporated into a monitoring system of
either camera or microphone. The practice of data collection and data processing is the specific nature of
these devices and their ability to monitor individuals at a granular scale. This technology is fundamentally
different from a standard security or CCTV camera.

Flock Safety: For the purpose of this Ordinance, Flock Safety shall refer to the company currently
providing the data collection and hardware for the ALPRs installed in the Town of York.

Section 4 ALPR Prohibited
Flock Safety shall comply with the following:
The Town of York shall cancel all Flock Safety contracts and services.
The Town of York shall have all Flock Safety hardware(s) and software(s) decommissioned and removed.
No ALPR system can be installed with public funding or inside York Town Departments without the
majority approval of the voters on the annually scheduled General Referendum.

Section 5 Effective Date
The ordinance shall become effective immediately following the approval of this ordinance.

ARTICLE SIX: Shall the Town authorize the use of up to $500,000 from unassigned fund
balance for the provision of ambulance and emergency medical services, and furthermore, shall
the Selectboard adopt this line item of the Town Budget, as proposed by the Budget Committee?
Funds for this expenditure would be taken entirely from the Fund Balance; there would be no tax
appropriation requested to fund this proposal.

Statement of Fact: The purpose of this article is to provide emergency funding for the
provision of ambulance and emergency medical services for the Town of York through
the FY27 budget year.

The Selectboard intends to provide up to $350,000 of this funding to York Ambulance
Association over the remainder of the fiscal year, if they are able to continue to provide
ambulance service to the Town according to the terms of the existing contract and the
additional conditions listed below.

In the event that York Ambulance Association is not able to continue to provide
ambulance services, the intent of the Selectboard would be to utilize up to the full
$500,000 amount to provide alternative ambulance and/or EMS service to the Town.

                           Recommended by the Selectboard: 5 - 0
                        Recommended by the Budget Committee: 5 - 2
The Town of York is currently in a 3-year contract with York Ambulance Association (YAA) to provide
ambulance and emergency medical services for the Town of York. The current contract is set to expire
on June 30th ,2027. In June of 2026 YAA approached the Town to request additional funding in order to
cover an anticipated budget shortfall for the current fiscal year through June 30, 2027. The Town has
been working diligently with our Public Safety Leadership, Town Finance Department, Town Manager’s
office, and YAA to evaluate and review the current request and explore all other options that may be
available to the Town to provide ambulance and emergency medical services.

The “conditions of funding” listed below are intended to serve as the general parameters of an agreement
that the Selectboard expects to enter into with York Ambulance Association.

CONDITIONS OF FUNDING:

It is the intention of the Selectboard to provide additional funding of up to $350,000 to York Ambulance
Association (YAA) between November of 2026 and June of 2027, at the sole discretion of the Town. If
provided, funding is expected to be based on actual financial need established through monthly financial
reporting from YAA to the Town of York through the Town’s Finance Director.

The calculation of such financial need shall take into account a comparison of actual expenses and
revenues to YAA’s forecast of expenses and revenues as of 08/13/26. The Town would expect to reduce
any additional funding by a corresponding amount should YAA’s actual revenues exceed revenue
projections. Examples of excess revenue include but are not limited to: unanticipated donations, gifts, or
grants, and run revenue/collections revenue that overperform forecasts.
