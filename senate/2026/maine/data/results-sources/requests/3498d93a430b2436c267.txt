June elections
Annual Open Town Meeting: Votes on municipal and school budgets
June 2, 2026 at 6:00 p.m.
Location: Conners-Emerson Gymnasium
This is the first portion of the Annual Town Meeting, where the school and municipal budgets will be voted on. The meeting continues Tuesday, June 10 with the Town Election of officers and all warrant articles designated for the secret ballot portion of the meeting.
Budget information page
A Citizen's Guide to Town Meeting, Maine Municipal Association, 2022
Town Election: Election of officers, votes on Land Use Ordinance Amendments
June 9, 2026
Polling Place: Municipal Building Auditorium
Poll Hours: 8 a.m. to 8 p.m.
The following offices will be elected by the voters at the June 9, 2026 Town Election:
Bar Harbor Town Council                              Three seats – Three-year term
Superintending School Committee                 Two seats   – Three-year term
MDI High School Trustee                               One seat – Three-year term 
Warrant Committee                                      Five seats – Three-year term and two seats - One-year term
Nomination papers are available in the Town Clerk’s office beginning Monday, March 2. Deadline for filing nomination papers with the Town Clerk is Friday, April 10, 2026 at 5:00 pm.  Call 207-288-4098 for more information.
Write-in candidates: Any qualified resident interested in being considered a write-in candidate for the town election must file a declaration of write-in candidacy with the Town Clerk on or before 5:00 p.m. on Thursday, June 4. The candidate must meet all the other qualifications for the office, and must receive a minimum of 25 votes to be elected.
The Town Election is a continuation of the Annual Town Meeting, which adjourns when the election results are announced. The warrant for the meeting includes information about any local ballot questions.

Declared Write-In Candidates:
For Democratic primary, Andrea LaFlamme of Bangor for US Senate and Brian Thomas of Lamoine for Judge of Probate. Any other names written in for any state or federal offices will be counted as blanks.
Ways to Request an Absentee Ballot
Applications for absentee ballots are available two months before the election (April 9). The ballots themselves will be available in early May.
By mail: Download the application and mail to Town Clerk's Office, 93 Cottage Street, Bar Harbor, ME 04609
By phone (voter may request for self only): call 207-288-4098.
In person in the Town Clerk's office (voter may request for self and/or immediate family members).
Online absentee ballot request service.
Last day to request an absentee ballot or vote in the Clerk's Office is Thursday, June 4.  Absentee Ballot envelopes must be received by close of polls, 8pm on Tuesday, June 9.
TOWN Ballot Questions
Article 2 – LAND USE ORDINANCE AMENDMENT – Holy Redeemer Church – Shall an ordinance, dated February 4, 2026, and entitled “An Amendment to amend the Official Neighborhood Districts Map by rezoning the Holy Redeemer Church from the Mount Desert Street Corridor District to the Downtown Residential District,” be enacted?
Article 3 – LAND USE ORDINANCE AMENDMENT – Design Review – Shall an ordinance, dated January 7, 2026, and entitled “An amendment to clarify the design review standards and process” be enacted?  
Article 4 – LAND USE ORDINANCE AMENDMENT – Campgrounds and Private Campsites – Shall an ordinance, dated January 7, 2026, and entitled “An amendment to definitions and standards for Campgrounds and Private Campsites” be enacted? 
Article 5 – LAND USE ORDINANCE AMENDMENT – Minimum Area Per Family – Shall an ordinance, dated January 7, 2026, and entitled “An amendment to remove the minimum area per family requirement in certain zoning districts” be enacted?
Article 6 – LAND USE ORDINANCE AMENDMENT – Lodging Regulations - Shall an ordinance, dated January 7, 2026, and entitled “An amendment to revise lodging definitions, establish minimum guest unit requirements and maximum transient guest limits for certain lodging uses, and remove some lodging uses from certain districts,” be enacted?
For more information about the proposed land use ordinance amendments, see the Land Use Information page.
