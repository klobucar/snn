Berwick Election Results Are In After Record June Turnout
Published on June 11, 2026
Berwick’s June election turned into a two-day count this week after more than 1,800 voters cast ballots in what town officials believe may be a record turnout for a June election in Berwick.
The municipal results were signed by the Town Clerk on June 11, following a long Election Day marked by steady lines, hot weather, a nine-page ballot, and the use of an auxiliary ballot box to keep voters moving.
In the Selectman/Overseer of the Poor race, Lisa Huestis and Lisa Chase were elected to the two available three-year seats. Huestis received 698 votes and Chase received 680 votes. Jeffrey Brooks received 382 votes, Noah Cobb received 310 votes and Ryan Michaels received 254 votes. There were 21 write-ins and 1,399 blanks.
In the School Board race, Margaret Wheeler was elected to a three-year term with 820 votes. Mathew Roy received 748 votes. There were 18 write-ins and 286 blanks.
Berwick voters also approved both MSAD 60 questions. The MSAD 60 District Budget Validation Referendum passed 981 to 804, with 44 blanks. The MSAD 60 District Referendum passed 1,161 to 703, with 55 blanks.
All local warrant articles passed.
Town Manager Arthur Capello said the turnout was far beyond what the town usually sees in June.
“We had what we believe to be a record turnout for the town for a June election,” Capello said. “Probably over 1,800 people turned out, which was a huge number of people.”
Capello said voters were lined up throughout the day, from the time the polls opened until they closed at 8pm. By the end of the night, he said, the line stretched nearly back to the parking lot outside the front door.
“It was a very well-attended election all day long,” Capello said. “From the moment the polls opened to the moment they closed, there was a line.”
The size of the turnout was made more complicated by the length of the ballot. Between state and local races and questions, voters were working through nine pages.
Capello said the town had expected a strong turnout, especially after the recent school budget public hearing, but not at the level Berwick saw Tuesday.
“Nobody ever anticipated a turnout like we had, which is wonderful to see,” Capello said. “I’m very happy and thrilled to have people come out and exercise their right to vote.”
To keep the line moving, the town used an auxiliary ballot box during the day. Those ballots then had to be processed after the polls closed, either by running them through the tabulators or by hand counting where needed.
Capello said Wednesday morning that the town had a rough estimate of about 800 ballots still to review as the count resumed.
“There were so many ballots that we had to bring out the auxiliary ballot box so we could keep the lines moving,” Capello said. “That means those all have to be run through the machine and or hand counted.”
One tabulator malfunctioned during the day, but Capello said no votes were affected. The state was contacted, a repair was made, and the machine was brought back online.
“It didn’t affect anybody’s vote,” Capello said. “That was one of the reasons why we also brought out the auxiliary box. We didn’t want to hold people up with just one machine working.”
The town resumed tallying Wednesday morning in the Burgess Room at Town Hall. The process was livestreamed and open to the public as space allowed.
When ballots are hand counted, Capello said, the town uses bipartisan teams made up of one Republican and one Democrat. Three teams of two were assembled Wednesday morning.
“When you’re doing a hand count, you have to have teams of two, a Republican and a Democrat,” Capello said. “Each party has to be represented.”
Each team reviews the ballots together and must agree on the tally. If there is a question about a ballot, the decision is referred to the election warden.
Capello praised Town Clerk Robin Patterson, who began in the role only weeks ago, along with the election staff and workers who handled the unusual election.
“I will compliment the staff,” Capello said. “They were extremely patient and extremely understanding, given the length of the ballot, the heat and the humidity, and the fact that the line was literally out the door almost in the parking lot all day long.”
Capello also thanked voters for their patience during a long and busy day at the polls.
“I want to thank all the residents and all of the workers for their due diligence,” Capello said.
