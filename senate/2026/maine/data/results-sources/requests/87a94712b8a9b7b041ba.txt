Voting Guide





Register to Vote:
Register to Vote Online
Absentee Voting:
Absentee ballots can be requested at the Maine.gov absentee ballot request page. This page is activated to receive ballot requests 3 months prior to each election. The Department of the Secretary of State developed this service in coordination with Maine's online service provider, InforME, with input from advocates at Disability Rights Maine. It is intended for voters with print disabilities, which may include vision impairment or blindness, physical dexterity limitations, learning disabilities or cognitive impairment, all of which prevent the individual from independently marking a paper ballot.
Voter Eligibility
In order to vote in Maine, you must first register to vote. You can vote in Maine if you are:
A citizen of the United States
An established resident in the town where you intend to register and vote
At least 18 years of age
You cannot be turned away from your voting place if you meet the requirements listed above. Register to vote online at this link.
Your Voting Rights
Registered voters are not required to show ID in order to vote.
You must be allowed to vote a challenged ballot even if you don't have ID or proof of where you live.
As of January 1, 2020, you may pre-register when you are 16, but you must be 18 years old to vote.
Registered voters who are 17 years old may vote in primary elections if they will be 18 by the general election.
Questions
Voters with questions can call the State's Elections Division in Augusta at 207-624-7650.
Voters can also call the toll-free election protection hotline, 1-866-OUR-VOTE. The Election Protection voter empowerment program is a coordinated nonpartisan effort to address obstructions of voting rights.

Political Sign Guidelines





According to Maine Statutes Title 23: Transportation Part 1: State Highway Law Chapter 21: Maine Traveler Information Services:
A temporary sign may not be placed within the public right-of-way for more than 6 weeks 
A temporary sign may not be placed within 30 feet of another temporary sign bearing the same or substantially the same message.
A temporary sign may not exceed 4 feet by 8 feet in size. 
A sign must include or be marked with the name and address of the individual, entity or organization that placed the sign within the public right-of-way and the date the sign was erected within the public right-of-way.
The Town of Freeport is committed to administering and enforcing applicable provisions of Maine law relating to political signage and election activities. Political signs placed within public rights-of-way must comply with the requirements of Maine law regarding placement, size, timing, and safety, and signs near polling places are subject to additional restrictions. 
The Town may investigate complaints related to sign placement and take appropriate action when violations of local ordinances or state law are identified.
To submit a complaint about signs, please use the form at this link.





Campaign Finance Notice





The Town of Freeport is not the agency responsible for regulating campaign finance activities, political spending disclosures, or campaign reporting requirements. Certain individuals, organizations, and advocacy groups that spend or raise funds to influence elections, candidates, referendum questions, citizen initiatives, or other ballot measures may be required to register and file financial disclosure reports under Maine law.
Questions regarding campaign finance laws, registration requirements, disclosure obligations, independent expenditures, political action committees, or ballot question committees should be directed to:
Maine Commission on Governmental Ethics and Election Practices
Phone: (207) 287-4179
Groups and individuals are encouraged to consult directly with the Ethics Commission to determine whether their activities require registration or reporting under Maine law. The Commission is the state agency charged with interpreting and enforcing Maine's campaign finance and election disclosure laws.
While the Town remains committed to fair, transparent, and lawful elections, responsibility for campaign finance compliance, reporting, and enforcement rests with the appropriate state authorities. The Town's acknowledgment of political activities, signage, or public communications should not be interpreted as a determination of compliance with campaign finance reporting requirements. Individuals and organizations participating in political advocacy are solely responsible for ensuring adherence to applicable state and federal laws.
