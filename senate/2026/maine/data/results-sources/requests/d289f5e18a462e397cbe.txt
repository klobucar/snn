Thoughtful About Berwick’s Future? This Is Your Moment • Select Board Papers Available Feb. 26
Published on February 25, 2026
Two seats on the Berwick Select Board will be on the ballot this year, and nomination papers become available beginning February 26.
According to Town Manager Arthur Capello, anyone interested in running can pick up papers from the Town Clerk starting that day. Candidates must gather at least 25 and no more than 100 signatures from registered Berwick voters. Signatures must be legible and include a valid Berwick address so the Town Clerk’s office can verify them.
Completed papers are returned to the Town Clerk for certification. Residents are encouraged to check directly with the Clerk’s office for exact deadlines and submission requirements.
The Select Board consists of five members elected to staggered three-year terms. The board meets on the first and third Tuesday of each month at 6:30pm. While meetings are typically held twice a month, additional meetings may occasionally be scheduled, particularly during budget season.
Capello described the role as both influential and collaborative.
“Being on the Select Board, in my opinion, you are the final say of the operations,” Capello said. “You have control of the budget once the legislative body, the voters, vote the budget in. The Select Board has the final say on how it’s used within the town.”
The board proposes the annual operating budget and warrant articles to voters. Department heads present their requests to the board, and members work through each proposal before forwarding a final budget to the community for approval.
“The departments bring what they feel is needed or proposed to the Select Board,” Capello said. “The Select Board will meet with each individual department head and determine what they feel is best for the community to move forward to the voters. Ultimately, the voters have the decision as to what gets approved and what doesn’t.”
In addition to budget oversight, the Select Board sets town policies and procedures and hires the Town Manager. Members also serve as a direct link between residents and town government.
“It’s an opportunity to bring your voice forward and have more of a voice in how the community is run,” Capello said. “It allows you to become more knowledgeable about the town and how each department operates.”
Outside of regular meetings, board members receive meeting materials the Thursday before each Tuesday meeting. Members are expected to review the packet in advance and come prepared with questions or feedback. The time commitment varies depending on the size of the packet and the topics under discussion, but Capello estimated it generally requires a few additional hours during the week outside of meetings.
Residents interested in running for a three-year term on the Select Board can contact the Town Clerk’s office for nomination papers and additional details about the upcoming election cycle.
