Upcoming Elections
November 3, 2026
The 2026 Maine gubernatorial election is scheduled to take place on November 3, 2026, to elect the 76th governor of Maine This election will coincide with a U.S. Senate election, U.S. House elections for Maine's two congressional districts, and various other state, county, and local elections. The primary elections were held on June 9, 2026.
UPDATED Polling Location: New Balance Field House & Memorial Gym ("The Big M")
Poll Hours: 7:00 a.m. – 8:00 p.m.
Sample Ballots: Municipal (PDF) and State (PDF)
View Notice: November 3 General and Penobscot County Referendum Election including Notice of Intent to Process Absentee Ballots, Licenced Facilities and Declared Write-ins.
Orono voters may register or update their voter registration at the Orono Town Office or any Maine BMV office. After October 14, registration must be completed in person. New voters must provide proof of identity and proof of Orono residency.
Absentee Voting: Absentee ballots may be requested online, by phone (866-2556), by application to the Town Clerk, or in person at the Town Office. In-person absentee voting will be available October 21–22, 8:00 a.m.–4:00 p.m. at the Town Office.
Absentee Ballot Request Deadline: October 29, 2026 (except for qualifying emergency circumstances).
Completed absentee ballots may be returned using secure drop boxes at the Orono Town Office or the UMaine Memorial Union.








 




Registration/First-Time Voting:
Orono residents may enroll, or make changes to their registration:
At the Orono Town Office at 59 Main Street, Monday - Thursday, 7:30am - 5:30pm 
At any State Bureau of Motor Vehicles Office while conducting other activities
On Election Day at the polling station, however advanced updates are strongly recommended
To register to vote for the first time, you will need:
Proof of ID (driver’s license, state or student ID, passport, birth certificate, or government-issued document)
Proof of residency (piece of mail showing an Orono street address)
Summary of Rules for Eligibility to Work in Maine Elections
State law sets the requirements for election workers:
Must be at least 18 years old
Must be a registered Maine voter
Must be a resident of the municipality or county where they will be serving
16- and 17-year-olds who are conditionally registered to vote are allowed to serve
Must not be a candidate or a member of a candidate’s immediate family
Expected Duties & Tasks
Although the work may vary slightly, the following duties are standard at many polling places:
Check in voters at the Incoming Voting List check-in table
Distribute ballots
Monitor the voting machine or ballot box
Help tally results at the end of the night
Benefits
There are several benefits of working at the polls:
Opportunity to help community and residents
Chance to visit and see neighbors, former co-workers, old friends that you may not have seen in a while
Learn more about the election process
Some positions are paid positions and while some are volunteer; this varies between communities. Some schools/colleges also give service credit for this type of volunteering.
